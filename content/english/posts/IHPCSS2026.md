From July 12 to 17, 2026, I attended the 16th International High Performance Computing Summer School (IHPCSS 2026) in Perth, Australia, as a Student Participant. The event was hosted by organizations including the Pawsey Supercomputing Research Centre.

## Why I Applied

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in computational neuroscience. Large-scale brain simulations need a lot of computing power, but my HPC skills were still growing. I found IHPCSS on my own while looking for ways to build up my skills. My main reason for applying was to see how other fields use HPC in practice, and to bring those ideas back to neuroscience. In the longer term, I want to run large-scale brain simulations using HPC, and IHPCSS felt like the right first step.

## Lectures and Workshops

The week-long program covered a wide range of topics. Two stood out to me in particular.

**Erik Lindahl's lecture on Software Engineering** was amazing. Beyond the technical content, his storytelling was outstanding—he pulled the whole audience in and made complex ideas feel clear and exciting. It reminded me that great science talks are just as much about how you tell the story as what you say.

**The Programming Challenge** was another highlight. I teamed up with a Ph.D. candidate from Canada, and together we worked on a problem under time pressure. Working with someone from a completely different background gave me new ideas that I would not have found on my own.

## Networking and Community

IHPCSS brought together participants from many countries and fields. What stood out most was how friendly and open everyone was, even though many of us—myself included—were not native English speakers. Through meals and social events, I got to talk with a wide range of people.

The **poster session** gave me a great chance to explain my research to people outside my field. The response was positive, but I also got sharp, open-ended questions that I could not fully answer—partly because of my limited English. That frustration has pushed me to improve before my next international conference.

## Looking Ahead

The biggest lesson from IHPCSS is a clear picture of how HPC can directly help my research. I plan to use the parallel computing and performance tuning methods I learned in my computational neuroscience work. The week I spent in the lively city of Perth also gave me a wider view as a researcher in ways I did not expect.

For anyone thinking about applying to IHPCSS: even if your HPC experience is limited, a desire to bring computing power to your own field is reason enough to go. I would strongly encourage you to give it a try.
